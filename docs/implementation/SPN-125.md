### What exists today

- A restore's evidence is two things under the private recovery root:
  `restore-jobs/<id>/state.json` (the journal record) and `restore-<id>/` (the
  encrypted source, raw originals, frozen copy, `failed-*`/`cold-*` captures and
  `original-<attempt>.sqlite`).
- `RestoreJobs.admit` refuses a 65th ID (`MAX_JOBS = 64`), counting failed and
  committed IDs alike. Nothing removes either part.
- `isCommittedRestoreJob` identifies resolved restores: phase
  `candidate-committed` (restored) or `original-committed` (recovered).

### Decision: tombstone-rename removal

Removal moves evidence out of addressable names with atomic renames and then
deletes the tombstones. A crash at any step is rolled forward by the next
supervisor run, which recomputes the same result without a separate plan file.

| Step | Durable action                                            | Crash cut name     |
| ---- | --------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------ |
| 1    | Create `restore-pruned/` (private, fsynced with the root) | `tombstone-dir`    |
| 2    | Rename `restore-<id>/` to `restore-pruned/<id>.evidence`  | `evidence-moved`   |
| 3    | Rename `restore-jobs/<id>/` to `restore-pruned/<id>.job`  | `job-moved`        |
| 4    | Delete the evidence tombstone, fsync the parent           | `evidence-deleted` |
| 5    | Delete the record tombstone, fsync the parent             | `done`             |

- The evidence moves **before** the record, so a half-finished removal is a
  resolved record without evidence, never evidence without a record. Reconcile
  finishes it: a resolved record whose `<id>.evidence` tombstone exists and whose
  `restore-<id>/` is gone has its job moved too.
- Every entry in `restore-pruned/` is by definition a tombstone and is deleted at
  the next run. An unrecognised name or a non-directory refuses the whole run.
- A record is eligible only when **all** of these hold, checked under the journal
  lock and serial queue immediately before the first rename:
  - the whole inventory lists and validates (any invalid record refuses the run);
  - no record is unresolved: every record is `failed` or committed;
  - the journal references no restore (`restoreJob` is null);
  - the record is committed and is not the journal's `completedOperationId`;
  - it is older than the newest **8** resolved restores (`RESTORE_EVIDENCE_KEEP`),
    ordered by `updatedAt`, then `intent.confirmedAt`, then ID;
  - `restore-<id>/` is a private directory, not a symlink, owned by this user.
- Policy: keep the newest 8 resolved restores, remove the rest oldest first.
  `failed` records (a restore that never replaced the workspace) are not
  resolved-by-commit, so they are kept and still count toward the 64-ID bound.
  Pruning them is a distinct decision and is deferred (see the ledger entry in
  the PR).

### When it runs

1. **Starting a new preparation** (`admitPreparedRestoreOwned`, a new ID only):
   prune before `jobs.admit`. A prune that throws (an unexpected state, such as an
   unrecognised tombstone) refuses the admission with "Restore evidence pruning
   refused an unexpected state." A prune that stops on an unresolved, referenced
   or ambiguous restore removes nothing, and admission then proceeds or hits the
   64-ID bound with its own message. Either way it never admits a restore on top
   of an unreconciled prune.
2. **Retention run**: supervisor startup, after restore reconciliation succeeds
   and no job is pending. A failure here never keeps the workspace closed; the
   next admission surfaces it.

### Crash-cut surface (every durable step)

Cuts 1 to 5 above, each interrupted by a real `SIGKILL` of a child process, then
reopened by a fresh process that must (a) leave every protected file
byte-identical, and (b) finish the removal and reach the same end state as an
uninterrupted run. Between the evidence move and the record move a resolved
record has no evidence; the next run finishes it, and it is never treated as
unresolved because only `failed` and committed phases are eligible.

## Persona weighing

- **Operator** (tipped it): bounded disk and an always-available restore.
- **Owner and Collaborator**: unaffected; no content or session changes.
- **Link guest**: unaffected; no sharing or access change.

## Test plan and evidence

Implemented in `src/server/maintenance/restore-jobs.test.ts` and
`supervisor.test.ts` (no separate test files):

- Focused cases: pruning order oldest first, equal-age tie broken by ID, the
  journal's completed restore kept, and refusal for unresolved, referenced,
  symlinked, file, missing, corrupt-record and unknown-tombstone inputs.
- 65th case: 64 records seeded, 56 removed, 8 kept with unchanged fingerprints,
  and the 65th admitted after pruning (refused before it).
- Real-process cuts: one child per cut (the five names above), killed with
  `SIGKILL` at the first root sync after that step
  (`restore-prune-crash-fixture.ts`), then a restart child finishes. Seeded
  committed records and small evidence trees, cloned from one real committed
  record; no browser restore per permutation.
- Supervisor wiring: a new preparation prunes first, an exact retry does not, a
  refused prune admits nothing, and startup retention never keeps the workspace
  closed.
- Supported Linux profile only (`/proc` descriptors): run in the Node 26 Docker
  image; the macOS run reports these as skipped, not passed.
- Limit: `SIGKILL` proves restart safety of the rename and delete order. It does
  not prove power-loss fsync ordering.

## Diagnostics

No new emitter. Pruning is an internal retention step with no operator-visible
event; a refused prune surfaces through the existing admission error. Operators
see the capacity and the refusal message in
[maintenance](../maintenance.md#resolved-restore-evidence).
