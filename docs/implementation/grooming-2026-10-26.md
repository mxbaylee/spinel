## Agent decisions and visual review

**Question:** Must a human approve product choices, designs or screenshots?

**Options:** keep individual human gates; renew delegation for each run; or give
agents standing authority while preserving independent evidence review. The owner
chose standing authority. Agents make product, design, implementation and sequencing
decisions by weighing Operator, Owner, Collaborator and Link guest, recording the
best supported option, alternatives, evidence and tradeoffs. Human sign-off is not
required. Remove `owner_input` from active tickets, the schema and generated index;
the checker rejects reintroducing the retired field.

Every UI PR embeds all proposed designs and actual current light/dark/phone
screenshots of affected screens in its body or conversation. Attachments or
durable commit-pinned image embeds are acceptable; CI artifact links supplement
the visible images. Identify the reviewed SHA, viewports, themes and states.
An independent nonimplementing subagent must inspect the images against
[the playbook](../design/README.md), theme tokens, artwork and all relevant
personas, record concrete findings and their resolution, and approve the visual
result. State **agent review only**. A grading reviewer can perform this inspection;
the existing two independent grades each above 95 on the same commit still apply.
No approval is inferred from the label, source code or a passing capture alone.

Use `visual-review` to request affected-screen captures. Nonvisual changes retain
the existing rendering/interaction checks without routine PNGs. Design proposals
and implementation evidence remain distinguishable; a fixture does not prove a
live flow. New patterns still enter the playbook through its prerequisite PR.

This supersedes human screenshot/artwork/flow sign-off in active tickets, the
run-specific limit in [decision 139](backlog-workflow.md#legacy-decision-139-run-specific-delegated-visual-approval),
the approval-authority clause of [on-demand captures](backlog-workflow.md#on-demand-storybook-captures),
and SPN-038's human timing/co-working gate. Historical approval records remain
provenance and do not reinstate these gates. Agents prepare launch/database/DNS
choices in the existing lifecycle order, with SPN-063 recovery before exposure.

Actual evidence and access are still required. Physical-device tests cannot be
replaced by viewport screenshots; credentials, upstream compatibility, existing
infrastructure and external-action authority cannot be assumed. Record a specific
unavailable capability and next action in ticket Rules and the PR checkpoint,
complete independent work and continue an eligible ticket. This grooming does
not deploy, provision paid resources or claim any unperformed test passed.

**Personas:** Operator gets fewer review stalls while retaining bounded resources,
honest verification and explicit infrastructure access. Owner gets decisions and
designs they can inspect on each PR without being a scheduling dependency.
Collaborator retains keyboard/touch, save and permission review. Link guest retains
scoped access, anonymity, revocation and unavailable-state review. All personas
are assessed even when a specific decision leaves one unaffected.

## Impact-first, larger-effort-first

**Question:** How should priority reflect impact and difficult work?

**Options:** retain small-effort preference; reverse effort without changing the
impact weight; or reverse effort and multiply impact points by ten. The owner
chose the last. Keep the existing four effort sizes and three impact sizes.

| Factor | S | M | L | XL |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Impact | 10 | 20 | 30 | — |
| Effort | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |

Own score is the sum, from 10 to 33. Each higher impact tier outranks every lower
tier's own score, and larger work comes first within an impact tier. Dependency
inheritance remains `max(own score, each dependent's score + 1)` through the whole
chain, so prerequisites can cross impact tiers. Lifecycle phase still sorts
first. Equal inherited scores prefer a prerequisite, then higher impact, larger
effort and lower ID. Regression cases cover all 12 combinations, all tie-breaks,
multi-level inheritance, lifecycle boundaries and the seven-column index.

**Personas:** All four retain their individual impact ratings; the maximum drives
priority. Operator/document/access protection receives its actual impact instead
of a discount for difficult verification. Owner/Collaborator larger improvements
no longer lose to polish merely because they are expensive. Link guest boundaries
stay part of acceptance regardless of ticket rank. Explicit owner scope and real
dependencies still govern which ticket an agent may work on.
