Replacing this with a short-circuit loop can remove that source-level callback creation/invocation mechanism while retaining the same graph traversal and predicate. However, **source-level function creation is not proof of a surviving heap allocation or material runtime cost**: the engine may inline or eliminate it, and a `for-of` form introduces iterator machinery with its own optimization behavior. The expensive descriptor/prototype/recursive work remains. Existing function/descriptor counts and anonymous CPU symbols do not isolate callback overhead, and the two inconclusive component runs do not supply that missing evidence. Do not describe this as an established allocation fix, a measured cause of the CJS slowdowns, or a remedy for the historical maxima.

Before production adoption, preserve exact boolean admission, own-key visitation order, descriptor-read and recursion order, short-circuit position, depth/prototype rejection and no accessor invocation. A static proof or callback-count control can establish equivalence and removed source operations. It cannot establish net speed. A future timing question, if separately proposed, should discriminate this particular mechanism on the retained real graph shapes and include loop machinery/negative results rather than automatically renewing the complete experiment.
